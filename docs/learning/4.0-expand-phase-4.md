## The mental shift

Slicing a frontend phase for someone who already knows the framework inverts the backend
phases' logic: there the product features were familiar (CRUD, jobs) and the *framework* was the
lesson; here the framework is home turf and the lesson hides in the deltas — what signals
replace (`computed` for derivation, `effect` almost never: the checkpoint question is the
phase's thesis), what `@if/@for` and `httpResource` make unnecessary, what zoneless forbids.
The steps are therefore sized by *concept*, not by screen count: 4.5 exists to make
`httpResource` + control flow the whole story of a read screen; 4.7 exists because the
proposal-review UX is where three phases of backend guardrails finally face a user.

## Gotchas hit

- The reference repo (`d:\Projects\MoD.HousingProject.Frontend`) is on the dev box, not in this
  container — the skill says "read the original first" for mirroring steps, so the expansion
  notes explicitly that comparisons happen on the dev box rather than pretending they happened
  here (the 3.4 honesty rule applied to step *planning*).
- Node is in the container but an Nx 21 + Angular 20 workspace creation needs registry access —
  probeable but not proven until 4.1 runs; the expansion keeps all environment risk inside 4.1
  (the 3.4/2.1 "environment work isolated in one step" pattern) instead of spreading npm
  assumptions across nine steps.
- "Signal forms" (the roadmap's word) are still experimental in Angular 20 — 4.6's step text
  keeps the term but the step will decide honestly between experimental signal forms and
  signals-interop reactive forms when it reads the installed version's docs, rather than
  promising an API that may not be stable.

## Check yourself

1. Why do i18n/RTL (4.2) and auth (4.4) come before any feature screen, when a demo would look
   more impressive the other way around?
2. Which two steps close UC-01, and what does each one prove that the other cannot?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. They are cross-cutting constraints every screen inherits: a screen built LTR-first with
   hardcoded strings or unauthenticated fetches has its layout, keys and data flow rebuilt when
   the foundations arrive — the frontend twin of adding the privacy wall after the model.
2. 3.7 (HTTP) proved the escrow contract — outcomes recorded, domain validation last, reminders
   resynced; 4.7 proves a *human* can operate it — warnings readable, edits natural, errors
   (`ExtractionNotReady`, cap) survivable. An API nobody can drive and a UI over a broken
   contract fail the same use case differently.
